Where the filter lives, and what the progress belongs to

Locked: the Library holds every title in every format, the resume rows sit above the list, and the four tabs are always there. Two things vary below, because they are the two that change how the screen reads: where the filter lives and how progress is attached to its row.

All four run the same model. Filters filter, search filters as you type, a row opens the player, and the ebook-only title opens the reader — which is under construction, so it says so. Turn the guides on in the bar below: one inset, one cover column, one tab-bar line.

A is cheapest, B is calmest, C is clearest, D drops filtering entirely.

A puts the filter words under the title, where they are read once on the way down into the list — no control to open, no state hidden behind a tap. It costs a row of height and it grows badly: a sixth filter wraps. Its progress line sits under the cover only, so the line is as wide as the thing it describes and can never be read as belonging to the row below.

B spends nothing: the filter is a value in the header, so the list starts higher and the screen is quiet. The cost is that the current filter is one word you can miss, and choosing one is two taps. Its progress rides inside the cover's bottom edge, lifted off the artwork by a hairline so it reads on a dark photograph and a light one. That is the real trade: five pixels of every cover are now chrome, and progress has left the reading order — you learn the number by looking at the picture rather than by reading the line under it.

C is the clearest: five words under the search field, one underlined. It reads as a place you are rather than a setting you made, which matters because a filtered library that looks complete is the real failure mode here. It costs the most height. Its progress line sits between the title block and the meta line, inside the text column, so it is bound to the text rather than to the row.

D refuses the filter. The list is grouped — In progress, Audiobooks, Ebooks — so the filters become headings you scroll past, and the only control is a sort. Nothing can be hidden by a filter you forgot you set. It costs scrolling, and it cannot answer "show me only the paired ones". Its progress is a percentage in the meta line and no line at all: unambiguous, since text belongs to the row it is in, and the slowest to scan.
