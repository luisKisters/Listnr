Four honest layouts for one player

Locked: title and author are always on screen, there is no Listen / Read & Listen segment at the top, and every control does something. Speed cycles 1.0→2.0×. Sleep opens 15 / 30 / 60 / Off and actually stops playback when it runs out. Chapters opens the real chapter list and seeking to one moves the scrubber.

The engine is real: one second of your time is thirty seconds of book time, multiplied by speed. Drag the scrubber, tap it, use the arrow keys on it. The skip buttons are 15 back and 30 forward; the outer two snap to chapter boundaries.

A hides the cover to show chapters. C never has to. D never hides anything.

A is the Apple Music shape: artwork at the top, identity under it, controls low where the thumb is. Because the cover is exactly as wide as the content inset, there is no room for a chapter list underneath — so Chapters takes the cover's place, which is the trade: you can see the artwork or the chapters, never both. For a book that is the right answer more often than for an album.

B is Audible's lineup with the segment removed and the identity moved above the artwork: title, author, cover, chapter, three times, five transport keys, three utilities. It is the most familiar and the most conservative, and it inherits A's problem — the chapter list still costs the cover.

C gives the cover the frame edge and everything else the inset, so the artwork is the screen and the controls are a quiet panel under it. A shorter cover buys back the room A spent, which is why C is the only one that can open the chapter list and keep the artwork. The cost is the one rule it breaks on purpose: the cover crosses the inset rails and lands on the frame edges instead.

D stops pretending the player is a place you look at. The book is a card, the chapter list is the screen, and playback runs underneath it. This is the layout for a non-fiction book you jump around in, and the wrong one for a novel you listen to straight through — the artwork is 64 px and the transport is small.
