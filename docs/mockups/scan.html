Scan a page, land on the second

You read the paper copy on the train and want the audiobook to carry on from there. Point the camera at the page: the text is read on the device, matched against a transcript of the audio, and one second comes back. You confirm it, then the player seeks there. Nothing jumps on its own — a wrong guess that moves the book is worse than a guess you can refuse.

Matching needs a transcript, so a book is transcribed once, on demand, in the background. That is the only work here with an honest fraction, and the only place a number is drawn. Everything works below: the first phone starts at the shutter and walks the whole loop — its shots alternate between a hit and a miss, so both endings are reachable — and the others open straight into the state they are named for. Turn the guides on in the bar and the viewfinder, the text and both actions land on the same two rails as every other screen in the set.

The five phases

Idle

The viewfinder is a dark box with a bracket frame, not a photograph: a rendered page would promise a fidelity nothing here has. The line above it names the book being matched, because a scan that silently searched the wrong book would explain none of its failures. One key, and a sentence saying what to point it at.

Reading

The frame freezes and dims, so the shutter visibly took. There is no progress bar: OCR and a fuzzy match have no honest fraction to report, and a bar that invents one teaches the reader to distrust every other bar in the app. It costs a second of silence and buys that trust.

Match

The snippet is quoted back first. The user judges the guess, not the app — three lines of the book's own words say more about whether the match is right than any confidence score would. Then the second and the chapter, and two actions: refuse on the left, confirm on the right. Refusing goes back to the viewfinder, not to an apology. Auto-jumping would save one tap and lose the position you were already at.

No match

A plain sentence, then what to do about it. The failure is named inside this book, because that is what actually happened: the text was read fine and found nowhere. A page of dialogue or a chapter heading gives the matcher almost nothing to work with, so the second line asks for more words rather than blaming the camera.

Not prepared

Scanning is worthless without a transcript, so the book that has none says so before the shutter, not after it. One sentence of why, one action. Preparing every book on import would burn an hour of battery for a feature most books never get pointed at.

Preparing

This is the one state that earns a number. Transcription walks the audio start to end and genuinely knows how far it got, so it counts in hours as well as percent, and the hairline is the same one the import read uses. It runs behind playback, which the last line says out loud so nobody sits and watches it.
